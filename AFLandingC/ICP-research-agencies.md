*Compiled 2026-07-22. *Deep-read set: Searlco x3, Jayger (customer), Loads of Traffic x3, Havas Market, 100.partners x2, Traffic Park, Fishbat, Adfuel, Astutr, Tandem8, Make It Bloom, Acquire x2, Block & Tam, Triton Growth (customer), MonsterClaw (customer), Novo Agency, IGG Marketing, Solutions by Sam, Dynamic Affiliate Marketing, Market Rocket x2, VML, plus 4 read-and-reclassified calls (1Veritas x2 and Upcode turned out brand-side; Traffic Family is a peer/advisory call). ## Who the agency ICP actually is (observed sub-segments)

1. **Specialist affiliate/partnership management agencies** – Searlco (Andy Reeve, ex Publisher Discovery enterprise, "some months will probably run 25 to 30 [brands] other months we'll get new clients who want 50 to 60"), Loads of Traffic (Nick Hubble/Jack Perks – "affiliate managers scattered around the globe", 78% gaming, ~31 geos), Acquire (Louis Jenkins – "between sort of 25 and 30 clients… our account managers only work on about four accounts at a time"), Tandem8 (Anthony Lynch, 20+ years, content-only programs), MonsterClaw (Rifat Ahmed – recruitment specialists with "set monthly recruitment targets"), Jayger (Jani Gysels – existing customer), Triton Growth ("we program manage affiliate programs pretty much and manage the recruitment, the reporting and all that"), Novo Agency, Solutions by Sam, Dynamic Affiliate Marketing, Market Rocket (9 client brands, ~600 outreaches per brand per month).
2. **Holding-company / full-service agencies adding affiliate** – Havas Market (Hamish Macgregor: "we'd be looking at using this across, you know, every single brand that we've got"), VML/WPP (Blue Bookhard: "a seemingly infinite number of brands that could be… brought underneath our umbrella"), fishbat (Scott Darrohn: "We could bring us to a lot of accounts"), Block & Tam (Stuart Weitzman etc.), Make It Bloom ("Bloom is the agency and then Surethink is one of our clients"), IGG Marketing.
3. **Partnership consultants / productized services** – 100.partners (Tyler Gillespie: done-for-you partner management education, now building an AI partnership-manager on AF data), Astutr (Jeet Dhindsa, Seoul – retainer consulting, Grin refugee).
4. **Adjacent/hybrid** – Traffic Park (publisher moving into program management: "the plan is also to manage affiliate programs in the future"), Searlco's Affillo network (white-label "Publisher Hunter" embed), Adfuel (multi-office media agency).

**How they buy:** almost every agency call follows the same ladder – test on themselves or one guinea-pig client → Growth (5 brands) → custom/enterprise across the roster. Phil names it explicitly: "What typically happens with agencies, and we've got a fair few of them, is they come on with one, test it, go on to the growth, extend it, and then they work out if they want to go in the enterprise or not."

## A. Top agency pains (ranked by frequency/intensity)

1. **Client rotation vs fixed brand slots** – the single most agency-specific pain. "the way that your pricing is sort of set up would mean that… we'd have to have a country and a user usually for a month and then move it for another month. Whereas we really need to sort of be in and out of all of the above" (Nick Hubble, Loads of Traffic); "One of my affiliate managers might be working with a sportsbook in Nigeria today… But then tomorrow they're focused on casino in Peru" (Nick); "agencies accounts are changing pretty fast… I'm like rotating clients with the current subscription… we just weren't able to like change that brand and now it's like, hey, we're not really even using it" (Gulraiz Farooqi, Triton Growth); "the 249amonth is good but you're only limited to five brands, right?… we have more than five brands in the agency" (Daniel Pasco, Adfuel); "I didn't know that I was kind of locked into that when I did the trial" (Suzanne Snider, Dynamic Affiliate Marketing).
2. **Manual per-client list building doesn't scale** – "we'd get our database together, find all the contacts, put them into a sheet and reach out manually" (Jack Perks, Loads of Traffic); "everything is very manual for finding affiliates. It's Amanda, myself, and one internal team member quite literally scraping the Internet for the best partners" (Sam Silverman, Solutions by Sam); "we go on Impact or awin… or we use Ahrefs for competitor analysis or even just like a Google search of competitors… it's from a lot of different places, basically" (Mariana, Novo Agency); "I spent about four hours on Go AF Pro a day and currently it's getting us nothing" (Aaron Higgins, Market Rocket); "We literally hire an entire other agency to help us communicate to creators because there's so much manual work" (Blue Bookhard, VML); "they literally just had like five Koreans all sitting next to each other… each one, like, searching a hashtag and then just manually DMing on Instagram" (Jeet Dhindsa, Astutr, about a client).
3. **Junk volume vs signal** – "There's no point having 10,000 results when 9,900 is s***. You may as well just concentrate on the hundred" (Andy Reeve, Searlco); "when we say credit, like I would say 60% are non relevant. And also a lot of YouTube like channel shows, highly relevant. But if you actually go into there, you would see that the last video was posted two years ago" (Rifat Ahmed, MonsterClaw); "I'm usually pulling from the same pool of people for every single client. Every time I do similar searches, we're getting the same people come up" (Isabella Slyman, Market Rocket); existing tools "do a good job of… showing you the kind of the whales… but the sort of nano and micro influencer is, if anything, becoming more valuable" (Blue, VML).
4. **Tool costs multiplied per client / stacked subscriptions** – "the commercials of the likes of Connecto and affiliate AI were just quite significant" (Hamish, Havas); "We've paid a lot of money for pro pro pro version of similar web… you're talking big books there" (Nick, Loads of Traffic); Grin: "It was like 24k a year" (Jeet, Astutr); Publisher Discovery: "I just didn't feel like it was any value because it was quite expensive as well… it'd be cheaper, more efficient for me just to use the A Win directory" (Anthony Lynch, Tandem8); seat-multiplication shock: "If it's one brand and it's 10 countries, is that classed as 10 seats?… Oh God. That's gonna end up being very expensive" (Nick, on ~$600/mo).
5. **Hard limits / hitting the ceiling mid-engagement** – "they used [it] for a month and asked to cancel because they got hit by the limit" (Rifat, MonsterClaw, about his own project managers); "What I don't want is limitations. They want to get to a certain point and we're stuck" (Andy Reeve); "I don't want to be paying for something I'm not going to be using but at the same time I don't want to be limited either" (Nick Hubble).
6. **Contact-data quality and dead ends** – "we got no responses from the emails, I don't know if these emails are verified or what" (Daniel Pasco, Adfuel); "there was one for GQ and one for Glamour magazine. I emailed them and they just got bounced back, said, no emails exist" (Anthony Lynch, Tandem8); info@ vs personal: "let's say we've only found with affiliate finder the info ads but I can find a James ad somewhere. How do I add James ad to the campaign?" (Jani Gysels, Jayger); "I've sent thousands of emails. Sometimes it got very few responses… I'm probably on a blacklist somewhere. Several, several blacklists" (Margaret Herrera, Dynamic Affiliate Marketing).
7. **Reporting to clients / proving ROI on shrinking budgets** – "at the end of the day our customers want to see return on investment… if we're reaching out, getting a load of affiliates, doing stuff but not making much in the way of sales… They're going to say what the hell" (Zena Scott, Market Rocket); "we're already battling budgets that are down 50% year over year" (Kellie York, Block & Tam); "Very, very low incrementality on them… it's trying to explain that to the brand when they're paying them like two or three grand a month in commission" (Anthony Lynch, Tandem8, on coupon/extension affiliates); "we go out to our clients and build reports on a go to market strategy. So we do a lot of that" (Nick Hubble).
8. **Recruitment quotas vs manpower** – "they have set monthly recruitment targets every month… to do 5000 UGC realistically I might have to discover and reach out to at least a hundred thousand potential influencers" (Rifat, MonsterClaw); "we have 17 clients in the pipeline and we're not being able to onboard them because I don't have like the manpower" (Rifat); "You have 600 per brand per month that you have to do, don't you Aaron?" (Zena, Market Rocket); "We don't want to get to a thousand clients and not have the staff or manpower to deliver" (Louis Jenkins, Acquire).
9. **Owner/admin burden of managing the tooling itself** – "it's going to be me that's gonna happen to manage that then on a subscription basis every month of upping this and downing that… I haven't got the time to do it… It's gonna be a nightmare for me if I'm honest" (Nick Hubble); "I haven't got to sit there and we've got to share three logins or something like that. It's just a nightmare" (Andy Reeve, on seats).
10. **Keyboard time vs relationship time** – "What I'm really trying to get my guys to do is remove the manual work… if they're not talking to affiliates, I want them talking, picking up the phone… Being a keyboard warrior and spending half of your time doing all the manual stuff doesn't build those relationships with affiliates" (Nick Hubble); "the recruiting process, there's a pain point there. You guys are obviously solving" (Tyler Gillespie, 100.partners); "lead pulling is kind of like straightforward, but then the real recruitment part of it or like getting them on a call, the outreach and things like that. Like there's obviously like variables there" (Gulraiz, Triton Growth).
11. **Incumbent tools breaking or failing silently** – GoAffPro: "it's just stopped working… Aaron's been in constant communications with them saying why isn't this app working any longer? And they can't provide us with a solution… they're paying for the premium version, believe it or not" (Zena, Market Rocket); Publisher Discovery: "we don't use their email platform because it's just not working… also it does have issues when pulling in data" (Andy Reeve); "We're using Goethe Pro to source affiliates. Aaron says it's crap, it doesn't work. Putting that in layman's terms" (Zena).
12. **Clients expect done-for-you and stay demanding** – "ultimately when we get people in they expect us to do it all for them. So it's our account managers that will go ahead and do it" (Andy Reeve); "Nine times out of ten, the merchants won't want to run anything because… they're just concentrating on their own business. That's why they need us" (Andy); "we couldn't set and forget… if we don't deliver it within a week then they're always sort of on our case" (Louis, Acquire); "we're kind of just using these recycled, you know, Rakuten Rewards and Capital One and they push volume… but I just don't want to default to those long term" (Kellie, Block & Tam).

## G. Pricing psychology

- **Delight zone: $99–$249/mo self-serve.** "the 249amonth is good but you're only limited to five brands, right?" (Daniel Pasco – price fine, cap is the friction); "You're not charging hundreds of dollars a month, so this makes it possible" (Chris Sammarone, Upcode); "It's worth the $100 a month for us" (Chris); "these costs are much lower than what we've seen in previous proposal" (Hamish, Havas, vs Connecto/Affiliate AI); "Your price point was very obviously reasonable and I was paying six [grand a year before]" (Paul Vassau).
- **The agency anchor is Publisher Discovery at ~£/$500/mo unlimited.** "what I'm looking to do is find a competitive project where we can pay 500450 to $500 a month that gives us what that enterprise level gives us" (Andy Reeve). Anything that beats that on quality at similar money wins the specialist agencies.
- **Fear zone: per-seat/per-geo multiplication.** 10 countries = 10 seats ≈ $600/mo: "Oh God… That's gonna end up being very expensive… I'm not arguing with you on the quality. That's adding up to quite a big number very, very, very quickly" (Nick Hubble). Unlimited-tool refugees fear meters: "on publish discovery, I'm unlimited… What you're saying is if I go over 10, I could be paying more than I am on publish discovery" (Phil, voicing Andy's objection).
- **Elasticity sells.** "If you drop two brands you reduce your price automatically… It'll scale up and down" → "Okay. Amazing. Brilliant. Lovely. That's great." (Phil ↔ Zena); "if you delete a brand you get billed one less. If you add two brands on, you get billed two more" (Phil to Searlco); "it's going to be sensible, it's going to be scalable whatever it is… we might not want to start with 30 [geos]… it might be 20… and it might be 40" (Nick Hubble).
- **Custom enterprise deals actually quoted:** Loads of Traffic: "a flat 29.25 per geo and then $0.07 per email enriched, double verified and custom AI sent" with a 20-geo minimum ("if you do 30 GEOs it's around eight 60" – Jamie); per-brand math repeated across calls: "Between $50 and $60 per brand" / "probably going to be like $45 per client per month" at 100 clients (Phil to Searlco); Havas quote: "$100 a month [1 brand/1 geo]… five brands… you're into 250amonth", beyond five "custom".
- **Agency margin math is the buying frame.** "if we're spending way more than what we're spending right now, it would not make sense. So I would expect like a deal which is sweet as well and leaves us profit to make money because end of the day we are not the end customer. We are just agency like and billing clients" (Rifat, MonsterClaw). Savings framing works: "I think we will save you more than you'll end up paying us… you'll be able to not pay for a cold outreach tool… the price might look bad, but it will be good when you look at the saving" (Jamie to Loads of Traffic).
- **Referral/reseller economics come up unprompted:** "if we were to partner with you guys, would there be a kind of like agency referral rate where we can offer them a slight like 10% or 20% off the rate card" (Louis, Acquire); "do you do like an affiliate for that or a finders fee?" (Jeet – answer: "30% for the software, 10% for agency fulfillment"); "instead of like we buying as an agency, we simply would ask the client to buy it… so then we make the commission and also use the software" (Rifat); Searlco negotiated half-price reseller terms for Affillo.
- **Credits are accepted once explained as refundable-on-failure:** "If we successfully find and verify an account, then that's the credit. If we can't find it, then we refund the credit" (Jamie to Adfuel); top-ups "$15 for 100" / "$10 for 100" quoted without pushback.

## Copy implications for agencies.affiliatefinder.ai

1. **Lead with the roster, not the brand.** The defining agency job is "roll out across every single brand that we've got" while clients rotate monthly. Hero should promise one platform for every client brand – add a brand when a client lands, drop it when they churn, price follows automatically ("scale up and down" is the phrase that got an out-loud "Amazing. Brilliant. Lovely.").
2. **Attack the per-client manual stack** – the observed status quo is GoAffPro/marketplace digging + Similarweb referral recycling + spreadsheets + a second tool for emails. "Four hours a day… getting us nothing" (Aaron) and "quite literally scraping the Internet" (Sam) are the pains to invert: minutes per client, same-day lists, verified contacts, outreach in the same tab.
3. **Kill the two fears in the same section: limits and meters.** Agencies arrive from unlimited tools (Publisher Discovery) terrified of hitting caps mid-engagement ("they got hit by the limit" – Rifat; "we're stuck" – Andy) AND of per-seat multiplication ("10 seats… Oh God"). Message: per-brand pricing ~flat, credits refunded when no email is found, no per-geo seat trap, custom enterprise when the roster is big.
4. **Price anchors to use:** Publisher Discovery ~$500/mo (and "six grand a year… wasn't worth it"), Grin $24k/yr, Upfluence "thousands a month", Connecto/Affiliate AI "commercials quite significant". AffiliateFinder's $99–$249 self-serve plus ~$50/brand agency math reads as a rounding error against one client retainer (their own retainers run $2k–5k/mo).
5. **Bake the agency buying ladder into the CTA.** Every agency wants to "test it out on ourselves first before we throw clients into it" (fishbat) with one guinea-pig client. The reps' standing offer on calls is a free agency trial (30 days, 5 brands, no card – "we're doing this for all agencies"). The page should mirror that: test on one client → roll out → custom deal. Do not gate behind annual contracts; the 12-month-commitment fear (IGG, Impact comparison) is live.
6. **Preempt the recurring buying questions in an agency FAQ:** How many client brands/seats? Can I swap brands when clients change? Can outreach send from the agency's name instead of the client's (template/sender override exists)? How are affiliates qualified (link/UTM detection)? What can I export? Is there onboarding for my account managers ("hand holding feature is quite important")? White-label: discussed on multiple calls and done for partners (Searlco/Affillo "Publisher Hunter") – position as "talk to us", not a self-serve toggle.
7. **Proof assets agencies asked for and didn't get on-page:** a shareable one-pager/"literature" for internal business cases (Kellie), response-rate numbers (25–32% claims drew "Wow"s but need substantiation before publishing), and agency-specific case studies. Kellie's frame is a gift for a testimonial pull-quote once permission is cleared: "like a creator IQ but for us in a more effective way."
8. **Tone: talk like an operator, not a SaaS.** Use their words – roster, geos, seats, retainers, incrementality, recruitment targets, account managers – and their enemy images: junk lists ("9,900 is s***"), the same recycled pool, keyboard-warrior admin, coupon polluters, set-and-forget agencies. Never promise features that are only roadmap on the calls (Amazon scraping, TikTok Shop sales data, per-geo drill-down, agency reporting module, MCP) – all were explicitly "coming", not live, at call time.
